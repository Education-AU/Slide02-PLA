Consider the vector space $\mathbb{R}^n$. The set
$$
B=\{ \mathbf{e}_1=(1,0,0\dots 0), \mathbf{e}_2=(0,1,0,\dots ,0 ),\dots , \mathbf{e}_n=(0,0,\dots ,1)\}
$$
is a basis. Notice it is finite


<h3 class="h3-blue">B is linear independent</h3>

$$
c_1\mathbf{e}_1+c_2+ \dots + c_n\mathbf{e}_n=\mathbf{0}
$$

$$
(c_1,c_2, \dots ,c_n)=\mathbf{0} \Rightarrow  c_1=c_2= \dots =c_n=0
$$
<h3 class="h3-blue">$\mathbb{R}^n=\textbf{Span}(\textbf{B})$</h3>

Fo any  $$ \mathbf{x}=(x_1,x_2,\dots ,x_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n$$

$$x=x_1\mathbf{e}_1+x_2\mathbf{e}_2 \dots +x_n\mathbf{e}_n$$

This is called the **standard basis**
