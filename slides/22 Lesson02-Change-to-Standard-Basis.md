We have now seen how to convert coordinates from one arbitrary basis to another arbitrary basis.

But it should be recognized that converting from some arbitrary basis to the **standard basis** is much simpler!

Why because we immediately know the coordinates of the initial basis in the standard basis.

This has an impact on practical calculations.

Consider some initial basis

$$
\mathbf{f}_1,\mathbf{f}_2,\dots ,\mathbf{f}_n
$$

and let the target basis be the **standard basis**.

$$
\mathbf{e}_1=\mathbf{ (1,0,0,\dots ,0)},\mathbf{e}_2=\mathbf{ (0,1,0,\dots ,0)},\dots ,\mathbf{e}_n=\mathbf{ (0,0,0,\dots ,1)}
$$

Let's assume we know the coordinates $c_j$ in the basis $\mathbf{f}_j$ and want coordinates $d_i$ in the standard
basis $\mathbf{e}_i$.

Then we can immediately write the coefficients $a_{ij}$

$a_{1k},a_{2k},\dots a_{nk}$, are the coordinates of the k'th basis vector in the target basis which is the standard
basis which in turn is simply the vector components of the k'th initial basis vector
