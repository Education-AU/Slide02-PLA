In this lesson we are going to define the concept of a **basis** and see how vectors can be represented by
**coordinates** in such a bases.

We are also going to study how to transform a representation from one basis to another. But first the notions of

1. **Linear Combination**
2. **Linear Independence**
3. **Linear Dependence**

<h3 style="color: blue;">Linear Combination</h3>
A vector $\mathbf{v}$ is said to be a linear combination of vectors $\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2 \dots ,\mathbf{v}_n$ if
for some scalars $ c_1,c_2 \dots ,c_n $

$$\mathbf{v}=c_1\mathbf{v}_1+c_2\mathbf{v}_2 \dots +c_n\mathbf{v}_n$$

<h3 style="color: blue;">Linear Independence</h3>

Let $V$ be a vector space. A subset $U\subset V$ is said to be linear independent if

For all finite subsets $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \dots, \mathbf{v}_n\}\subset V$

$$
c_1 \mathbf{v}_1 + c_2\mathbf{v}_2 \dots + c_n \mathbf{v}_n = \mathbf{0} \Rightarrow c_1=c_2= \dots = c_n =0
$$
We saw earlier that this implied uniqueness of coordinates!!
