Consider two bases of $\mathbb{R}^2$:


$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{e}_1=(1,0),\quad  \mathbf{e}_2=(0,1)\\
\mathbf{f}_1=(1,1),\quad  \mathbf{f}_2=(-1,1)
\end{aligned}
$$
And a vector 

$$
\mathbf{v}=c_1\mathbf{e}_1+c_2\mathbf{e}_2
$$

We know that the coordinates in the new basis $\mathbf{f}_1, \mathbf{f}_2$ are calculated by

$$
d_i=\sum_{j=1}^2 a_{ij}c_j
$$

And that for fixed $j$ the $a_{1j},a_{2j}$ are the coordinates of the vector $\mathbf{e}_j$ in the basis $\mathbf{f}_i$

Or formulated in words:

The new coordinate for $\mathbf{v}$ in the basis $\mathbf{f}_i$ is calculated by 

Calculating the sum of the old coordinates times every coordinate of that index $i$ of the old basis in the target basis summed up.
