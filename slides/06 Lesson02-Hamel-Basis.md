<h3 style="color: blue;">Hamel Basis</h3>

Let $V$ be a vector space. Then the set $B\subset V$ is a basis of $V$ if

1. $B$ is independent
2. $\operatorname{span} (B)=V$, or equivalently every vector $\mathbf{v}\in V$ can be expressed as a linear combination
   of the vectors in $B$

#### Remark:

The term **Hamel basis** is useful to know when reading mathematical literature. It is used to distinguish the usual
concept of a basis in a vector space from the concept of a **Schauder basis**, which is used in topological vector
spaces.

The term **Schauder basis** is a more specialized concept that additionally involves the topology of the vector space
and allows **infinite** convergent expansions. It will **not** be discussed in this course.
