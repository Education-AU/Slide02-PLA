So, given a matrix $A$ that maps coordinates the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$ maps the other way, which is not surprising.

This can be exploited in general coordinate changes between any two bases.


Consider the vector space $\mathbb{R}^n$ with bases

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{b}_1,\mathbf{b}_2,\dots \mathbf{b}_n\\
\mathbf{f}_1,\mathbf{f}_2,\dots \mathbf{f}_n
\end{aligned}
$$

And remember we have the standard basis
$$
\mathbf{e}_1,\mathbf{e}_2,\dots \mathbf{e}_n
$$
Let's say we are given coordinates in basis $\mathbf{b}_i$ and we want to change to coordinates in basis $\mathbf{f}_i$
